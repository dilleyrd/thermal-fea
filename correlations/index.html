Common relations (Theory §8)¶

Source of every correlation in §8 and §8.1, unless a row names another: [I] Bergman, Lavine, Incropera and DeWitt, Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer, 7th ed., Wiley, 2011. The equation numbers below are the book's (owner's decision, 2026-11-23).

h = Nu · k_f / L_c. Fluid properties at the film temperature T_film = (T_s + T∞)/2 unless the correlation states otherwise. T_s is the wall temperature of the previous nonlinear iteration (lagged), either local (at each integration point; default) or the area average over the boundary set (option).

Re = ρ V L/μ, Pr = μ c_p/k_f, Ra = g β |T_s − T∞| L³ Pr ρ²/μ², g = 9.80665 m/s². For ideal gases β = 1/T_film; for liquids β comes from the property table.

Validity ranges and warnings¶

For the horizontal-plate pair, pick "up" or "down" automatically at run time from the sign of (T_s − T∞) and the declared face direction. Outside a validity range: still evaluate, and emit one warning per boundary condition per solve. plate_forced_local needs the leading-edge point and flow direction; x is the distance of the integration point from the leading edge along the flow direction (minimum x = 1e-6 m).

inclined_natural: when the face direction is declared, a face for which [I] makes no recommendation counts as outside the validity range: the top face (up) of a hot plate (T_s > T∞), or the bottom face (down) of a cold plate. When no face direction is declared, this is not checked.

Characteristic lengths in a 2D model.

  • Horizontal plates. [I] defines L = A_s/P (Eq. 9.29). In a planar model a horizontal face is a strip of unit depth, so A_s/P is half the strip's width; [I] Example 9.3 uses w/2 in the same way. In an axisymmetric model, a horizontal ring from r_i to r_o gives A_s/P = (r_o − r_i)/2. Either way, the automatic L_c of hplate_up_natural and hplate_down_natural is half the total length of the selection. This settles Q17.2.
  • Other correlations. For the other plate correlations and the vertical channels (channel height), the automatic L_c is the total length of the selection.
  • Diameters. The cylinder, sphere and non-circular cylinder correlations need L_c (the diameter or D) given.
  • An explicit L_c always overrides the automatic one.

Extra inputs (Data §8 params):

  • Vertical channels: S, the plate spacing [m].
  • slot_jet: W, the slot width [m]; H, the nozzle-to-surface distance [m]; x_r, the half-width of the region averaged over [m]. The default x_r is half the total length of the selection.
  • round_jet: D, the nozzle diameter [m]; H [m]; r_r, the radius of the region averaged over [m]. In an axisymmetric model the default r_r is the largest radial coordinate of the selection; a planar model must give r_r.
  • The jets take their L_c from these inputs, so a given L_c is refused.

Internal duct flow, friction factor and pressure drop (Theory §8.1)¶

This is the text of §8.1 of the theory document, unchanged. It holds the laminar Nusselt numbers, the turbulent and transition relations, the optional Dittus–Boelter relation, the Churchill friction factor and the pressure drop of a duct.

Re = ṁ D_h /(A μ), D_h = 4A/P_wet. Properties at the bulk fluid temperature.

  • Laminar (Re ≤ 2300): Nu_lam from the tables below ([I] Tables 8.1 and 8.2).
  • Turbulent (Re ≥ 4000): Gnielinski ([I] 8.62, with f from 8.21) Nu = (f/8)(Re − 1000) Pr / [1 + 12.7 (f/8)^½ (Pr^⅔ − 1)], f = (0.790 ln Re − 1.64)⁻² (valid 0.5 ≤ Pr ≤ 2000, 3000 ≤ Re ≤ 5e6).
  • Transition (2300 < Re < 4000): linear interpolation in Re between Nu_lam and Nu_Gnielinski(Re = 4000). [I] gives no transition correlation; this rule is the program's.

Default is the constant-wall-temperature value. Optional user selections, each replacing Gnielinski as the turbulent formula (the laminar value and the transition rule are kept):

  • Dittus–Boelter ([I] 8.60): Nu = 0.023 Re^0.8 Pr^n. n = 0.4 if the wall is hotter than the fluid, else 0.3. Valid for 0.6 ≤ Pr ≤ 160 and Re ≥ 1e4.
  • Sieder–Tate ([I] 8.61): Nu = 0.027 Re^(4/5) Pr^(1/3) (μ/μ_s)^0.14. μ_s is taken at the cell's mean wall temperature T̄_w; the other properties at the bulk temperature. Valid for 0.7 ≤ Pr ≤ 16700 and Re ≥ 1e4. [I] recommends it for flows with large property variation, such as oils.

Friction factor, all regimes (Churchill 1977, not in [I]), roughness e: f = 8 [ (8/Re)¹² + (A + B)^(−1.5) ]^(1/12), A = [2.457 ln( 1 / ((7/Re)^0.9 + 0.27 e/D_h) )]¹⁶, B = (37530/Re)¹⁶

Pressure drop of a duct of length L with minor-loss coefficient K_minor: Δp = (f L/D_h + K_minor) · ρ V²/2, V = ṁ/(ρ A).

§8.2 Correlations of [I] that are not used¶

Recorded so that no task adds them without a decision. On 2026-11-23 the owner judged the correlations in use adequate and declined the enclosure, mixed-convection and Table 8.1 f·Re additions (Q30.4):

  • Circular cylinder, Hilpert ([I] 7.52, Table 7.2) and Zukauskas (7.53). These cover the same case as Churchill–Bernstein (7.54), which is kept. Horizontal cylinder, Morgan (9.33, Table 9.1) is not used either; Churchill–Chu (9.34) is kept.
  • Liquid-metal forms: the flat plate (7.32) and fully developed tube flow (8.64, 8.65). Mercury is the only liquid metal built in. Other correlations used outside their Pr range warn as usual.
  • Flat plate with uniform surface flux (7.45–7.48) or an unheated starting length (7.60–7.64). The surface conditions these assume are not known to a load.
  • Tube banks (7.58, 7.59), packed beds (7.81), falling drops (7.57), and arrays of jets (7.73, 7.77). These need array geometry and a mean fluid temperature that a single-surface load does not have.
  • Laminar entry lengths (8.57, 8.58). These give a Nusselt number averaged from the inlet to x, but the stream needs a local value in each cell.
  • Colebrook friction (8.20) and laminar f·Re of non-circular sections (Table 8.1). Churchill 1977 is kept; it applies to circular ducts and to D_h. Using Table 8.1's f·Re would need a rule for joining it to the turbulent branch, which [I] does not give (OPEN_QUESTIONS Q30).
  • Isoflux vertical channels (9.46), inclined channels (9.47), and the isolated-plate optimum spacings (Table 9.3). The isothermal form 9.45 is used.
  • Enclosures: rectangular cavities, concentric cylinders and spheres (9.49–9.63). They couple two surfaces across a cavity, and a convection load sees only one ambient temperature (Q30).
  • Mixed free and forced convection (9.64). This needs two correlations in one load (Q30).
  • Boiling and condensation (chapter 10). The fluids here do not change phase.

The stream and network equations that use these relations are in Theory §9.

Literature sources¶

Every correlation except the user power law comes from Bergman, Lavine, Incropera and DeWitt, Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer, 7th ed., Wiley, 2011; each entry above gives its equation numbers. The Churchill friction factor (1977) is not in that book. The correlations of the book that the program does not use, and why, are listed in Theory §8.2.
